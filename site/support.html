Questions about Textstufe

Why does “Hallo” not get a level?

A single word says almost nothing about the difficulty of a text. Textstufe requires at least 50 words in three sentences before it assigns a CEFR band. Shorter input receives an explanation instead of a misleading grade.

How is the level calculated?

Textstufe uses the published Zurich ZIX model: mean sentence length, long-word ratio, vocabulary coverage, and word frequency. The six features are standardized and passed through the published Ridge regression, then mapped to a rough CEFR band. The app adds a minimum length, German-language detection, and an uncertainty band.

This is a pragmatic reading indication, not an official CEFR test. It must not be used for grading, exams, admissions, or consequential decisions.

What does the ZIX score mean?

The score ranges from -10 to +10. Positive values indicate easier texts, negative values indicate harder texts. It is not a percentage and not a grade.

Does the app need internet?

No. Analysis runs entirely on the device. Only the optional donation screen asks the App Store for localized product prices.

Which languages are supported?

The analysed text must be German. The interface follows the device language and is available in English, German, French, Spanish, Italian, Polish, Russian, Ukrainian, Japanese, Simplified Chinese, Portuguese, and Turkish.
